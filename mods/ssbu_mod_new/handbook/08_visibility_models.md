Anything without a tier below is A/B unless marked otherwise. Paths in the source column refer to the *old* workspace (`modpack_test_1`); the same material now lives under `projects/*/history/`, `dumps/` and `evidence/`.

- `param_visibility_data_body` stores per-state **mesh names lowercased** (`hash40("alarmm")`), group/state names exact case. This decoded 124/134 hashes; the 10 left are the `0x7FB997A80` empty sentinel. `lhand_alarm`=`AlarmM`. Brute-force shortest hashes first; hash40's high 32 bits are the length. (B; fix is A "looks normal") - a workaround; keep `build.py` docstring note (stale) in mind.

- Adding a mesh needs all four model files consistent (numdlb, numshb, numatb, nusktb) + bounding sphere + visibility track. (Shulk fair, A)
- Aegis sword: 48 eflame scripts call `generate_article(ESWORD)` and 142 call `add_motion_partial`, while exactly one (`Lose.txt`) ever hides it - so **no per-move guard or visibility call can cover the spawn sites** (R-18), and the guard that tried is dead. The sword is hidden through the mesh it is drawn from: every one of the article's eight clips (`fighter/eflame/motion/esword/<slot>/`) carries a `Visibility` track with a single `flamesword` node, and in vanilla it is **True in all eight** - including the retracted `close` state that a freshly generated article sits in, which is why one spawn kept the sword on screen for the rest of the match. `tools/build_esword_pose.py` (0.15.1) ships c06/c07 copies with that track False in `close`, `to_close` and the four `d01specials*` clips and True in `open`/`to_open`, so a sword that exists but is not being swung is not drawn. The state table is asserted at build time against the article's own `motion_list.bin`, including that entry 0 is `close`. **The Chrom jab additionally needs its own drawn states** (`jab_open`/`jab_to_open`, `Sword1` at 0.9 against the fair's 1.4) because the two moves share one article and one bone; those two entries were appended with the wrong animation-hash convention until 2026-10-03 - see the next line. The **angle** is a separate problem and is still wrong. Workaround to retire by porting the remaining Pyra specials. (B offline; console result pending)
- **An article-level visibility call does not make a mesh appear; the mesh's own visibility track in whatever clip the article is actually playing does.** The two are separate levers and the track wins. Measured on the eflame esword, 2026-10-03: `set_visibility_whole(true)` plus `add_motion_partial("jab_to_open")` drew nothing for four releases (0.20.0-0.20.20, user report) because `build_esword_pose._build_jab_mlist` wrote the appended entry's animation-name hash as `hash40('jabtoopen')` while **an animation-name hash covers the file name *including its extension*** - every vanilla entry of that article proves it (`open` -> `0xe32317ab0` == `hash40('z00open.nuanmb')`, `close` -> `0xf40969975` == `hash40('z00close.nuanmb')`). The name resolved to no file, which is silent: no error, no log, the article keeps playing `z00close`, and `z00close`'s `flamesword` track is False, so it hid the blade the ACMD had just asked for. The Cloud fair was fine throughout because it drives the vanilla `to_open` kind, whose name was never wrong. Two rules: (a) `add_motion_partial`'s anim argument matches the entry's **motion kind**, never the clip name, so only *cloned* entries are exposed to this; (b) every animation hash a generator writes must be checked to resolve to a real file - `tools/audit_chrom_jab_sword.py --check` does this and reports 10 failures against the broken baseline, 0 against the fix. (B)
- **An article is posed and sized by its own motion clips, not by the fighter's.** The esword's clips animate `HaveR`, `Sword1` and `Sword_flameA..E` and never `Root`. A `Sword1` track added to the *body* fair clip changed nothing on hardware (R-21), and so did scaling the model's `Root` bind basis (R-20). Consequences: the grafted body bone named in the fair's ACMD is not what holds the blade; the attach chain runs owner `HaveR` -> the article's own `HaveR` -> `Sword1`; and the blade's axis must be read from the esword mesh instead of assumed. `tools/fair_sword_geometry.py` still models the refuted assumption and now says so at the top - **its numbers are not evidence.**
- **"The body cannot re-pose the blade" does not mean "the body cannot move it."** The fair clip animates the grafted `HaveR` across all 80 frames and the esword rides it; the jab clip leaves `HaveR` at a single constant local transform, which is still enough because `HaveR` is a child of `HandR` (`HandR -> HaveR -> Sword1 -> Sword2` in the built c06/c07 skeleton) and `HandR` moves through 42 distinct rotations in `c00attack11.nuanmb`. So a constant-local `HaveR` means the blade and the `sword1` trail *follow the hand*; it does not mean they are frozen - and a bone with one sample is not evidence of a static limb in this tree. (B, measured 2026-10-03 on the shipped c06/c07 clips.)
